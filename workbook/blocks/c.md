# Section C — Power and switching (your section)

**What it does.** Everything the instrument switches, and the power that lets it. Your section takes 5 V from USB-C or the barrel jack and makes every rail the board needs; it takes a separate **external supply** (7–18 V) for the parts that move real current; it switches four relays and reads two isolated digital inputs; and it carries the two NMR switches — an **H-bridge** that reverses the current in a field coil, and a **MOSFET** that switches a polarizing coil carrying amps. If your section is wrong, nothing else works; if it is right, nobody notices — until the demonstration's "alarm → relay → notification" step, which ends here.

**Parts in your zone (≈ 75, plus the power-entry sheet you read but do not edit).**
- **Rails (the instructor's sheet — read it, do not edit it).** USB-C receptacle and DC005 jack, a polyfuse and a Schottky per input for ORing, a TVS, two isolated 5 V → ±12 V modules with their ferrites and capacitors, a 78L05 for +5VA, an AMS1117 for +3V3, four rail LEDs and a test point per rail. This is the sheet a student error would brick a board with, so the instructor owns it — but the rail rule is yours to know: **anything that is not voltage-sensitive runs from raw +5V or +3V3** (LEDs, the dev board, relay coils, logic buffers); ±12 V and +5VA feed **only** the analog parts.
- **The external power input (yours).** A 2-pin screw terminal for 7–18 V DC, a 5 A fast-blow fuse, an SMBJ26A TVS after it, a P-channel MOSFET as reverse-polarity protection with its 47 kΩ gate resistor and a 12 V Zener that keeps the gate inside its rating, 100 µF + 100 nF of bulk, and a green LED with its resistor. It feeds the transmitter's power stage in Section B, the H-bridge and the polarizer switch — nothing else.
- **Relays and isolated inputs (yours).** 4 × **Hongfa JQC-3FF/005-1ZS** relays — 19 × 15.5 mm, five pins (two for the coil, then COM, NO and NC), contacts rated **10 A at 277 V AC** — with their coils on raw +5V (5 V, 70 Ω, about 71 mA each), driven through small MOSFETs (AO3400A) from the I²C expander that lives on Section B's sheet, each coil with a flyback diode across it; a yellow LED + 2.2 kΩ per channel; 3-pin screw terminals COM/NO/NC. **These four channels are designed to switch mains** (110 V AC wall power). The board's rating is **250 V AC, 5 A maximum per channel** — the copper sets that, not the relay — and **the load must have its own fuse of 5 A or less**. Mains work happens only with the instructor present, the lid on and the load fused, never during class; the everyday case is still low voltage, ≤ 30 V DC. 2 × 6N137 optocouplers, each input LED fed through a series 1N4148W, a 220 Ω resistor and a two-transistor MMBT5551 current limiter (with its 10 kΩ bias and 100 Ω / 1 kΩ emitter set) so that 5 V and 24 V give the same LED current; +3V3 and a 1 kΩ pull-up on the output side, outputs to GPIO 16 and 17 (a GPIO is a general-purpose pin on the microcontroller).
- **The H-bridge (yours).** **DRV8871** (6.5–45 V, 3.6 A) from the external supply, with a 100 nF and a 100 µF at its supply pin, a 32 kΩ 0.1 % resistor that sets the current limit, 10 kΩ pull-downs on both inputs so the bridge coasts at reset, an SMBJ26A across the supply, and a 16 A screw terminal out to the field coil. Inputs `HB_IN1`/`HB_IN2` = GPIO 9 and 14.
- **The polarizer switch (yours).** A **UCC27517** gate driver and an **AOD4184A** MOSFET (40 V, 50 A, 8.5 mΩ, TO-252) with a 10 Ω gate resistor, a 10 kΩ pull-down so the switch is off unless driven (`FET_GATE` = GPIO 47), a 16 A 3-pin terminal for the coil, and three ways of absorbing the coil energy: two of them on the board, on one set of footprints — an SS54 freewheel diode (fitted by default) or an SMBJ20A fast-dump clamp — and the third, the RC snubber (2.2 Ω 5 W + 4700 µF), wired externally across the coil terminal, because those two parts are too big for the board.

**Reference numbers.** 2xx = power entry and rails · 4xx = relays and isolated inputs · 9xx = the coil switches and the external power input. Keep them: the footprints on the board carry the same names.

**Your zone on the PCB** (the outlined region of the physical board that is yours to route)**.** `ZONE_C`, the rear of the board: power entry along the rear-left edge, the four relays in a row along the rear edge with each screw terminal directly behind its own relay, the coil terminals at the rear-right, the optocouplers at the far end from the relays. The rear edge is the back of the instrument, so **every terminal on it takes its wire straight off the edge** — nothing points inwards. **The isolation band:** the optocoupler input side (terminals, resistor, diode, LED pins) is the `ISO_IN` net class (a net is a set of pins that are connected together; a net class sets rules for a group of them) with **2.5 mm clearance** to everything and no copper of any other net under it on any layer — the design rule check tests it, and so does your reviewer. **The mains band:** the relay contacts — COM, NO and NC, from the relay's contact pins to the screw terminal — are the `MAINS` net class, because these channels may carry wall power. That copper keeps **5 mm from everything else on every layer**, **stays on the top layer with no vias**, and is **3 mm wide**; the terminal sits directly behind its relay so the run is short, and each channel carries its rating on the silkscreen. The design rule check enforces all of it. *Rule of thumb: mains copper touches nothing else and never changes layer.* The high-current traces (the H-bridge outputs, the polarizer drain, the external supply) are wide: at least 2 mm, no neck-downs at pads, vias or connectors. The thermal pads of the H-bridge and the switch sit on ground copper with a 3 × 3 array of vias under them.

## The two questions (E3, between workshops) — short answers that show the *why*
1. **What happens to the energy stored in a coil when you switch its current off?** *(think: ½LI² for 900 µH at 13.4 A; the three ways the design can absorb it — a diode, a resistor-capacitor snubber, a clamp — and what each does to the turn-off time)*
2. **Why is the optocoupler input side allowed to have no ground connection to the rest of the board — and what would break if someone poured ground under it?** *(isolation, creepage, and what a 2.5 mm gap is actually worth)*

## Your design number (E3)
**Switching the polarizer coil.** Take the reference case: a coil of 900 µH carrying 13.4 A, to be switched off in about 10 ms. Compute (a) the stored energy, (b) the snubber resistor that keeps the peak drain voltage below the MOSFET's 40 V rating from a 12 V supply, (c) the capacitor that gives the wanted decay, (d) the check that the circuit is over-damped, and (e) the energy the resistor eats per shot. Then the second half: the brief originally specified an SMBJ58A clamp — look up its breakdown and clamping voltage and say, in one sentence, why it protects nothing here. *Expected magnitude:* 81 mJ; R < 2.09 Ω, so 2.2 Ω 5 W; C ≈ 4700 µF; over-damped, yes; 81 mJ per shot at a few hundred watts for a few milliseconds — and a clamp that only fires at 94 V is decoration in front of a 40 V transistor. Working, a unit and a one-line conclusion into `docs/students/<name>/notes.md`.

## Your software (E11)
Commands `relay {n, on}`, `relay_all {on}`, `opto_reset`; status `relay1…relay4, opto1, opto2` (edge counts) and `opto1_level, opto2_level`. Then the part only your section can do: the **expander driver** (the relays hang off the I²C expander, so a relay command is now a register write, not a pin write), the **H-bridge** (`field {dir, ms}` — forward, reverse, coast, never both inputs high), and the **safety interlocks**: read the power stage's current and thermal flags after every pulse and refuse to keep pulsing when one is set; leave every switch off at reset; never leave the polarizer on without a time limit. ## What this section teaches
- **Switching an inductor is an energy problem with three answers** — dissipate it slowly, dissipate it fast, or store it — and the board carries two of them on one footprint set, with the third wired externally, so you choose with numbers.
- **A clamp voltage above the transistor rating is decoration.** Protection parts are chosen against the thing they protect, not against the supply.
- **Isolation is geometry.** Creepage is a distance on the board, and one careless pour defeats it — 2.5 mm at the isolated inputs, 5 mm once a contact can carry mains.
- **The copper sets the rating, not the part.** The relay's contacts are good for 10 A, the board's tracks for 5 A, so the channel is 5 A — and the protection for that number lives in the load's own fuse.
- **Current has to return somewhere.** Amps of coil current returning through the wrong copper is how a microvolt receiver stops working.
- **Reverse-polarity protection, fusing and ORing** — the unglamorous circuits that decide whether a board survives its first afternoon on a bench.
- **An interlock is part of the design**, not a line you add after something burns.
