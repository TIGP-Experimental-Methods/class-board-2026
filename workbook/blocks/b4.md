# B4 — Isolated switching (your block)

**What it does.** **Four relays** (SPDT, 30 V DC / 1 A, never mains) the phone can switch, and **two fast isolated digital inputs** (6N137 optocouplers) that accept 5–24 V signals from other equipment without sharing ground. It is the instrument's muscle and its ears for the outside world; the demo's "alarm → relay → notification" step ends here.

**Parts in your zone (≈ 24).** 4 × HK4100F-DC5V relays with coils from raw +5V driven by a **ULN2003A** Darlington array (inputs from GPIO 4, 6, 7, 15; its COM pin to +5V gives the flyback diodes); a yellow LED + 2.2 kΩ per channel; 3-pin screw terminals COM/NO/NC; 2 × **6N137** with 1 kΩ series + 1N4148 anti-parallel on the input LED, +3V3 on the output side with 100 nF, 1 kΩ pull-up, outputs to GPIO 16, 17 (active low); 2-pin terminals for the isolated inputs.

**Schematic.** Full PDF page *B4*. Your **gapped sheet** is missing one relay channel (relay, LED, 2.2 kΩ, 3P terminal) and one opto input network. Your **LCSC part** (E8): the HK4100F relay, C12072.

**Your zone on the PCB.** `ZONE_B4`, rear-right: relays in a row along the rear edge with the terminals right behind them; ULN2003 between relays and the socket; optos at the far end from the relays. **The isolation band:** the opto input side (terminals, 1 kΩ, diode, LED pins) is the `ISO_IN` net class with a **2.5 mm clearance** to everything else and no copper of any other net under it, on any layer. DRC checks it; your reviewer checks it too.

## The two questions (E3, HW1) — short answers that show the *why*
1. **What does the diode across each relay coil do?** *(it lives inside the ULN2003 — find the COM pin; think about what a coil does when its current is switched off suddenly)*
2. **Why is the opto's input side allowed to have no ground connection to the rest of the board — and what would break if someone poured GND under it?** *(isolation, creepage, and what "fast" means for a 6N137)*

Harder pair for L2: which pins carry the fastest signal in your block, and how long is the pull-up trace? Where do the relay contact currents flow — do they touch any signal ground?

## Your design number (E3, 15 min)
**Can all four relays be on at once?** HK4100F-DC5V coil: 0.36 W → coil current; ULN2003 V_CE(sat) at that current (datasheet curve) → dissipation per channel and for four; compare with the SOIC-16 package limit at 25 °C and at 50 °C inside a closed box. Second number: with a 2.5 mm isolation band, look up IEC 60664-1 (pollution degree 2, material group III) and state the working voltage that clearance/creepage supports — how much margin over the 24 V input rating? *Expected magnitude:* ≈ 72 mA per coil; ≈ 0.3 W for four channels — fine, but say by how much; 2.5 mm supports a few hundred volts basic insulation. Into `notes.md`.

## Your number (E5 → E13b)
**Relay switching time**: wire relay 1's NO contact to opto input 1 through a 5 V supply; command the relay from the phone; the firmware timestamps the opto edge. Expected 5–10 ms.

## Your software (E11)
Commands `relay {n, on}`, `relay_all {on}`, `opto_reset`; status `relay1…relay4, opto1, opto2` (edge counts) and `opto1_level, opto2_level`. Real hardware writes are plain `digitalWrite`s — your block is the first with real GPIO. SIM: fake opto edges every few seconds. Alarm rule: `opto1` count jump → `notify`. Pair with B5 in L3: their TTL line into your opto.
